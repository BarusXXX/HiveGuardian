The Maltese honey bee

Apis mellifera ruttneri

A dark honey bee endemic to the Maltese islands. Foragers, guards, nurses, and builders are roles of the same worker bees. Protecting food and brood helps the whole colony.

Meet Malta’s native bee ↗
❋

A living food chain

Nectar becomes honey. Pollen supports nutrition and brood. Workers secrete wax, and nurse bees produce royal jelly. Bees resting on stone do not collect pollen there.

Honey bee biology ↗

Last sting is a gameplay sacrifice. In nature, worker honey bees do not necessarily die when stinging other insects.

How honey bee stings work ↗
✿

A Maltese spring

Look for sagħtar, lellux, poppies, borage and rockrose among the limestone. Capers trail over the rubble walls. The blossoming żebbuġ is an olive: its tiny cream flowers offer pollen. Olive is wind-pollinated, so these flowers provide no nectar.

◇

Defense is a balance

A restricted entrance and protective mesh help guards, but traffic and ventilation matter. Electric harps can mitigate hunting pressure. No single device guarantees protection. Correct identification and professional nest response matter.

Research on harps and muzzles ↗
⌁

Know each hornet

The Asian hornet, Vespa velutina, has a dark body and yellow lower legs. Malta’s native Oriental hornet, Vespa orientalis, has a reddish body and bold yellow markings. The optional giant challenge uses Vespa mandarinia: a much larger hornet with an orange head and several broad orange abdominal bands. It is distinct from Vespa velutina. Other insects are part of the ecosystem.

Malta’s Oriental hornet ↗ · Identify the giant hornet ↗
⬡

Seal, lure, capture

Seal damaged hive timber first. A meat lure can draw interested hornets away; its inward funnel cage holds them while bees escape through the mesh. Real traps can also catch other insects. Perfect bee escape and death after one compressed game day are game rules.

Apiary capture traps and selectivity ↗
Imagine the future. Understand the present.

The pictured laser turrets, mirror dome, and treated-pellet dispenser are speculative game technology. Their fictional chemistry is not real beekeeping guidance. Collective grounding and accelerated brood development are gameplay abstractions.
